Is it safe? Security and privacy

Plain answers for anyone who has heard "open source" and wonders whether it is safe to keep their business books in it: where your data is, what the app connects to, and how to check it yourself.

The short answer

Your invoices, clients and settings are saved as files on your own computer. There is no account, no login and no cloud server of ours. The app has no ads and no tracking, and it sends none of your business data to us. Every line of its code is public, so anyone can check this.

Don't take our word for it: switch off your internet and use the app (it all works), or turn on Offline mode so it never goes online by itself. See Check it yourself.

#Your data stays on your device — with real AES-256-GCM Vault Encryption

  • 100% Local-First Storage. In Web / PWA mode, your books live in your browser's persistent IndexedDB storage on your own device; in Desktop server mode, they live in the local data/ folder on your PC (bound strictly to 127.0.0.1).
  • Real WebCrypto AES-256-GCM Encryption Vault. When you enable the App PIN & Encrypted Vault in Settings → Privacy & Security, your books are encrypted at rest using browser-native AES-256-GCM with a key derived via PBKDF2-SHA256 (310,000 iterations), paired with optional WebAuthn Fingerprint / Passkey unlock, Multi-Staff Role PINs (Owner, Billing Staff, CA / Accountant), and a downloadable 24-character offline Recovery Key.
  • Encrypted .fgsb Backups. Exported backup archives can be password-encrypted with AES-256-GCM so copies on USB pen drives or cloud folders stay protected too. (In unencrypted Desktop folder mode without Vault PIN enabled, local JSON files follow your Windows/macOS user-account permissions.)
  • No account, no login server, no cloud database of ours. Bharat GST Free Billing never asks for an email or phone number to start, and we operate zero servers that receive your business data.
  • Google Drive only if you connect your own account. If you choose to connect Google Drive (Settings → Data & Backups → Google Drive), copies of invoice PDFs and backups go directly to your Drive. See Google Drive.

#It works without internet

Everything you do day to day works offline: making and printing invoices, PDFs, payments, stock, purchases, expenses, reports, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B files, backups, and reading a supplier's bill from a photo (OCR). The app runs on your computer and opens in your browser, but it is not a website.

Internet is needed only to install the app, to update it, and for the optional things listed below. When you are offline the app simply skips them; your work is not affected. Everything the app needs to run, even its font, is inside the app folder.

#Offline mode: never go online by itself

Click the Wi-Fi button at the top right of any screen and then Turn Offline mode on, or tick Offline mode in Settings → Privacy & internet. The button turns orange, with a line through it, while it is on. From then on the app makes no internet connection by itself:

  • no automatic check for a new version;
  • no reading of the supporters list;
  • no automatic copy of each invoice PDF to Google Drive.

Things you click still work when you click them: Check for Updates and Update, the ? help pages, Report, WhatsApp and other links, and Save to Drive. The setting is kept in the app's data folder, so the app's own server obeys it too. The same card lists everything that uses the internet, and when.

#No tracking, no ads, nothing sent to us

  • No analytics. The app does not count your clicks, screens or invoices, and does not report how you use it.
  • No ads, and no paid version that unlocks features.
  • No data sent to us. We never see your invoices, clients, amounts, GSTIN or bank details. We do not even know how many people use the app.

#Why is it free? What is the catch?

There is no catch, and your data is not the product.

  • It stays free, forever. There will be no paid version, no locked features, no ads and no tracking. And because the code is open source under the MIT licence, the app you have today can always be used, copied and kept by anyone, even if we stopped working on it. Nobody can take it away or start charging you for it.
  • It costs very little to keep going. The app runs on your computer, so there are no servers and no cloud bills for us to pay.
  • Maintained with Daily Continuous Auto-Updates. Bharat GST Free Billing is created and maintained by Abhishek Agrahari for businesses across India, shipping continuous improvements and safe idle auto-updates while keeping every single feature 100% free forever. See About & Support.
  • Tracking you would gain us nothing. We do not sell ads or data, so there is nothing to collect. Collecting your data would also break the one promise the app is built on.
  • The code is public, so this can be checked, not just believed.

#Other computers cannot open it

The app has a small built-in server that your browser talks to. It listens only on this computer's own internal address, 127.0.0.1, so other computers on your Wi-Fi or office network cannot open the app or read your data.

Unless you turn on phone access. With Use on phone or tablet on (Settings → Privacy & Security), the app also answers your own Wi-Fi, over an encrypted connection, and only devices you paired with a QR code and your PIN get in. It is off until you turn it on, and turning it off cuts every phone off at once. See Use on a phone or tablet and how it is kept safe.

It also refuses requests from other websites: a web page you visit cannot quietly read your books from the app in the background.

A computer shared by several people with their own Windows, Mac or Linux logins: the address 127.0.0.1 belongs to the whole computer, so someone signed in with another login on the same computer at the same time could also reach the app while it is running. Use the app on a computer only you (or people you trust) sign in to, and turn on the PIN lock. A login checked by the app's own server is planned with the secure phone and network access.

This is also why several people cannot use it from different computers at the same time. See Several people, or several computers.

#Can I put it online, on Railway, Render or a server?

Not yet, and please don't try. People ask whether they can host the app on a free website service (Railway, Render, a VPS, with Supabase as the database) so they can open it from anywhere. Today that is not safe, and it would not work properly:

  • There is no login on the server. The app was made for one computer, so its server does not ask who you are. The PIN only locks the screen in your browser. Put online, anyone who found the web address could read, change or delete all your invoices, clients and bank details.
  • It only listens on your own computer (127.0.0.1, see above), so a hosting service cannot reach it. Changing that in the code removes the protection described above.
  • Supabase would not be used. The app does not use a database. It saves your books as files in its data folder, and on most free hosting plans those files are wiped every time the service restarts.
  • The "free" plans are not free for long. Most now give a trial credit and then charge every month, plus extra for storage that keeps your files.

Hosting it online would also send your books to someone else's server, which is the one thing this app promises not to do.

What you can do instead: keep the app on one computer and take a backup often. To move to a new computer, see moving to a new computer. Using the app from a phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi works: see Use on a phone or tablet (encrypted, paired with your PIN, which the app's server checks). Using it over the internet may come later, but only after the app has a proper login of its own; this page will say so when it does.

#Check it yourself

You don't have to trust what we write. Any of these takes a few minutes and needs no technical skill except the last two.

  1. Switch the internet off. Turn off Wi-Fi (or unplug the cable), close the app and open it again. Make an invoice, download its PDF, print it, record a payment and open Reports. Everything works. That is because nothing the app does with your books needs the internet.
  2. Turn on Offline mode (above) and leave it on. The app then goes online only when you click something that needs it.
  3. Watch its connections. On Windows, open Resource Monitor (press Windows key + R, type resmon, press Enter), open the Network tab and look at node.exe, the program the app runs on. With Offline mode on, it has no connections outside your computer. On a Mac, use Activity Monitor's Network tab. Your browser is listed separately: the app's page uses it only for the things in the list above.
  4. Block it in the firewall. In Windows Defender Firewall's Advanced settings, add an Outbound rule that blocks the app's node.exe (in the app folder's _system\node folder, or C:\Program Files\nodejs). The app keeps working; only the update check stops. Remove the rule to update.

#What "open source" means here

Open source means the full program code is public. Bharat GST Free Billing's code is on GitHub under the MIT licence. Anyone (you, your IT person, a developer friend, a security researcher) can read every line and see exactly what it does. Nothing is hidden.

That is the opposite of a risk: a closed program asks you to trust it, while this one lets you check it. Many people look at the code, and problems they find are reported in public.

How a careful user can check

  • Read the code. The places to look: server.js (the built-in server, and the only outgoing call it makes: the update check), src/ (the screens), src/index.html (the list of addresses the page may contact), and install.ps1 / install.sh (the install line).
  • Read the install line before running it. Open the address in the install line in your browser: it is a plain text script you can read.
  • Watch the network. Your IT person can watch the computer's connections while the app runs and compare them with the list above.
  • Build it yourself. The release ZIP is made from this same public code with one command, npm run release:zip. A developer can run the app straight from the code (npm install, then npm start) instead of using our ZIP.

#Is the install safe?

  • No administrator rights. The install line puts the app in your own user folder (Windows: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Bharat GST Free Billing; Mac: Applications in your home folder). It does not change Windows system files, add a Windows service or ask for an admin password.
  • From the official place only. The install line downloads the script and the app from this project's own GitHub page, over a secure (HTTPS) connection.
  • Your data is never touched. Installing over an existing copy backs up your data to Documents\BharatGST Backups first and replaces only the app's own files.

Why Windows or the Mac may show a warning for the ZIP

The app is not code-signed. A signing certificate costs a few hundred dollars every year, and this app is free. So when you open the downloaded ZIP's launcher, Windows may show "Windows protected your PC" and a Mac may say it cannot check the app. That message means "this program is not signed by a paid certificate". It does not mean a virus was found. See "Windows protected your PC".

The one-line install shows no such warning because Windows and macOS put that check only on files marked as downloaded from the internet. Browsers add that mark; PowerShell and Terminal, which the install line uses, do not. It is not because the app was approved or certified by Microsoft or Apple; it was not.

#Backups and the PIN lock

  • Daily automatic backups. Every day the app copies all your data into a dated backup and keeps the last 30 days. See Daily backups.
  • A copy on another drive. Choose a second folder (another drive, a USB disk, a synced folder) and every daily backup is copied there too, so a failed hard disk does not take your books with it. See Keeping a copy somewhere else.
  • Before every update, the installer and the updater back up your data first.
  • PIN lock (optional). On a computer others use, turn on a 6 or 8 digit PIN in Settings → Privacy & Security → App lock. The PIN is stored only as a scrambled check value (a salted hash), never as the digits, and after five wrong tries in a row it makes you wait 30 seconds. It locks the app's screens; it does not encrypt the files. See App lock.

#Problem reports

When something goes wrong, the app notes it in the Problem log on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere by itself.

Only when you click Report does it open a GitHub problem form in your browser, filled in with the error and your app, browser and computer type. Before that, private details are removed: GSTIN, PAN, phone numbers, email addresses, UPI IDs, IFSC codes, long numbers such as bank account numbers, and your Windows user name. You see the form before anything is posted, and it is posted only if you submit it on GitHub.

#Updates and security fixes

  • Regular updates. A new version usually comes out every Saturday, with urgent fixes sooner. The app tells you when one is ready; see Updating.
  • Watched for known problems. GitHub's security alerts (Dependabot) watch the code libraries the app uses, and a known security problem in one of them is fixed in an update.
  • Only what the app needs. The download contains the built app and only the packages its server needs to run. Developer tools are left out.

#What you should do

  • Use only your official app bookmark: open Bharat GST Free Billing directly from your installed app or saved bookmark.
  • Keep Windows, macOS, or Android up to date, and keep built-in device security enabled.
  • Enable AES-256-GCM Vault & PIN Lock in Settings → Security if other people share the same device.
  • Keep an encrypted backup copy on a USB drive or Google Drive (see Settings → Data & Backups).

#Found a security problem?

Use synthetic sample data (never real invoices, GSTINs, or bank numbers) when documenting any security or bug report in the app's Problem Log.
